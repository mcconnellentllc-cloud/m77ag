Buy the Predator 459cc, item 58383. At 15.8 HP it matches the Vanguard's 16 HP, the 1 in. shaft with a 1/4 in. key means the existing pump coupler slides straight on, and it costs half what the V-twin does.

Move up to the 670cc V-twin only if the pump is rigidly direct-coupled and you want the smoother twin, or the wagon has been short on power under a full hopper. It is physically larger and weighs 110 lb, so measure the compartment first.

4. Measure Before You Buy

Five checks. Each one is a return trip if it is wrong.

Crankshaft length

Measure from the block face to the end of the shaft. The Predator is 3.5 in. If yours is the 2-29/32 in. Vanguard, the new shaft sits over half an inch proud and the coupler will need a spacer or the shaft will need shortening.

Base bolt pattern

Both Predators use the Honda-style 7.7 in. by 4 in. pattern with 11 mm holes. The Vanguard V-twin does not. Plan on drilling the mount plate or fabricating a new one.

Pump adapter

If a bell housing bolts to the engine's PTO face to carry the hydraulic pump, that bolt pattern will not carry over from the Vanguard. Measure the bolt circle and hole size before ordering.

Charging output

The 459cc has a charging coil in its parts list, but Harbor Freight does not publish the amp rating. Some 16 HP Vanguards carried a 16 amp alternator. If the Avery Weigh-Tronix head and the hydraulic solenoids run off the onboard battery, confirm the charging output covers the load, or plan to charge the battery off the wagon.

Exhaust and controls

The muffler sits in a different place on a single-cylinder engine, and the throttle and choke cable routing moves with it. The current exhaust and cable setup will not bolt over.

Call for the wagon manual first. The caution decal on the control door lists 800-291-5487 for a replacement manual. With model PK-302618 it will give you the engine mount plate and pump coupling dimensions on paper, which beats measuring blind and turns three trips to the store into one.

5. Setup Notes After the Swap

Governed speed

Set the governor to 3,000 RPM. The control door decal reads 3000 RPM MAXIMUM. The 459cc is rated at 3,600 RPM and free-runs to 3,800. Out of the crate it will overspeed this wagon's hydraulic system. Set it before the first load goes through the auger.

Oil

SAE 10W-30 above 32 F, 5W-30 at 32 F or below. Capacity is 1.16 quart on the 459cc. It has no oil filter, unlike the Vanguard it replaces, so the old NAPA Gold filter and its mount come off with the engine. The 670cc takes 2 quarts and does use a spin-on, Fram PH4967 or STP S4967.

Fuel

87 octane or better, treated with stabilizer. The wagon sits between harvests, so treat the fuel and shut the valve at the end of the season.

Battery

The Predator manual specifies a 12 volt, 300 CCA, 36 AH lead-acid battery, not included. Connect the negative cable to one of the engine mounting bolts per the manual.

Break-in

Run the first tank at light load, then change the oil. A new engine shedding assembly debris into a fresh crankcase is the cheapest maintenance there is.
