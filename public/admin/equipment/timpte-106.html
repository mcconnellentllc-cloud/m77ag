Brake lights not working: test steps

  1. Build air and hold the service brakes on (helper on the pedal or the trailer hand valve). Confirm the truck's own brake lights work.
  2. At the truck's 7-way socket, with the brakes on, check for 12 V on pin 4 (red). On most trucks pins 3 (yellow) and 5 (green) also carry stop when not signaling. The rear lamps on this trailer are combined stop / turn / tail.
  3. No voltage at the truck socket: the fault is on the truck. Check the stop light air pressure switch, its fuse or relay, and the turn signal switch. Hook up a second truck to confirm.
  4. Voltage at the socket: check the cord both ends for a bent or corroded pin, then the nose box for 12 V on the red terminal with the brakes on.
  5. Power at the nose box but lamps dark: trace the red, yellow and green circuits to the rear junction and the lamp plugs. Tail and turn signals working means the ground (pin 1, white) is likely good.
  6. Both sides out together points to the circuit, not the lamps. One side out points to that lamp, its plug or its pigtail.
  7. Pin 4 also backs up ABS power, so a stop circuit fault can set the ABS lamp.
  • Truck air compressor: governor cut-in 105 psi; cut-out recommended above 120 psi. Trailer brakes are weak if the truck's compressor is set too low (FMVSS-121).
  • No additives in the air brake system.
  • ABS power from the truck: the trailer ABS needs constant power on the center auxiliary pin (pin 7) of the 7-way whenever the key is on (FMVSS-121). Trucks built on or after March 1, 1997 supply it; an older truck needs the center pin wired to key-on power. Make sure that pin will not switch on other trailer equipment.
  • ABS lamp: if it comes on and stays on when braking a moving truck, the trailer ABS is not working; have it serviced after the trip.

Pre-trip check

  • Brakes: slack adjusters and pushrods free, chambers and air lines secure, no air leaks; drums not cracked or worn past the stamped maximum diameter.
  • Tires: 100 psi cold on all eight, matched duals, no cuts or bulges in the sidewalls.
  • Air ride: Neway Anchorlok control box behind the front of the tandem. Switch on for the road, gauge showing pressure, bags full and the trailer level.
  • Hopper gates: both gates closed and the rack-and-pinion openers latched before loading; crank shafts and gears free of grain and rust.
  • Tarp: roll tarp closed and latched, roll tube seated in every tarp stop, no tears, crank secured.
  • Lights and reflective tape: marker and clearance lights working, replace the cracked red rear marker lens; replace the faded and missing conspicuity tape on both sides.
  • Glad hands and pigtail: seals good, no air leaks with the brakes applied, 7-way and ABS plugs seated and secure.
  • ABS lamp: comes on at key-on and goes out; if it stays on while braking, service the trailer ABS.
  • Landing gear: raised fully and the crank handle stowed before moving.
Seen in the photos: the reflective tape along the lower rail is faded and partly missing on the driver and passenger sides, and the hopper gate gears show surface rust. Worth fixing before harvest hauling. The tire photographed shows weathering and fine cracks in the sidewall rubber near the size marking; check all eight tires for sidewall checking and the DOT date code (tires over 6 to 10 years old are due for replacement regardless of tread).
