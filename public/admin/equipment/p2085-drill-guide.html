1. Digi-Star Seed Tank Scale

Weigh bars under the seed tank feed a Digi-Star ISOBUS scale module. The module has no screen of its own. It shows on the tractor's John Deere display through ISOBUS, where it appears as Sensor System - Digi-Star, LLC.

Confirm the module model. The wiring and manual details below are for the Digi-Star Scale Link SL2 ISOBUS module (SL2110 or SL2140), the Digi-Star ISOBUS scale module that matches this setup. Read the model and part number off the label on the scale box on the drill and record them here before relying on the pin numbers.

3. Troubleshooting: Scale Drops Off the Display

Symptom on October 12, 2026: the John Deere display shows the disconnected-plug icon with Sensor System - Digi-Star, LLC and the scale screen is blank. Unplugging and reconnecting the black wire brought it back for about 5 minutes, then it dropped off again.

What the symptom means. The disconnected-plug message means the scale module itself stopped talking on ISOBUS. A bad weigh bar or load cell cable gives a wrong or unsteady weight, but the module stays on the screen. A module that drops off the display has lost power, lost ground, or lost its ISOBUS connection.

Most likely cause: the ground circuit. Black is the system ground on the module's power connector. Reseating it restored the scale for a few minutes, which points to a loose, corroded or broken ground connection that makes contact only briefly. Start there.

Step 1. Check the display's view of the connection

  1. On the John Deere display, open Status Center and the ISOBUS Controllers page. It shows whether the Digi-Star module is connected.
  2. If the scale comes back at any point, run DAN 1997 on the scale screen. It shows the module's supply voltage and an estimated load cell count (SL2 manual, page 27). Record the voltage in the log.

Step 2. Ground and power at the scale box (most likely)

  1. Tractor running, scale dropped out. Back-probe the 6-pin connector at the scale box: red (pin 1) to black (pin 3). It must read 11 V or more. Below 11 V the module stops sending data.
  2. Leave the meter connected and wiggle the harness from the scale box back toward the hitch. If the voltage drops out while you move it, the break is where you were wiggling.
  3. Measure from black (pin 3) to the tractor battery negative while running. A good ground reads close to 0 V. A reading of several tenths of a volt or more means a bad ground connection between those two points.
  4. Open the Deutsch connector. Look for a pin pushed back in its cavity, a missing or loose wedge lock, green corrosion, or water. A pin that backs out under pressure makes contact only until the connector moves. Seat the pins, replace the wedge lock if loose, and apply dielectric grease.
  5. Follow the black wire to its other end (ring terminal, splice or harness connector). Check the crimp and the mounting point for rust or paint under the terminal.

Step 3. The harness run from the tractor

  1. Inspect the harness at the hitch, the drill's fold and hinge points, and anywhere it crosses the frame. Look for chafed insulation or a pinched spot.
  2. Check the ISOBUS breakaway connector at the back of the tractor is fully seated and locked.
  3. Check the ISOBUS terminator at the end of the bus is in place and tight. A loose terminator usually drops every ISOBUS device, not only the scale.

Step 4. If power and ground hold steady and it still drops off

  1. Check the yellow (CAN High) and green (CAN Low) wires for the same damage as above.
  2. Check for water inside the scale box and the junction boxes.
  3. Unplug the module's power for 10 seconds and reconnect (SL2 manual, page 16). After a power cycle the scale screen can take several minutes to reload on the display.
  4. If nothing else has changed on the ISOBUS, an address conflict is unlikely. If another ISOBUS device was added, change the scale's Preferred Address on its System Setup screen (page 27).

Weigh bars (only if the scale stays on screen but the weight is wrong)

  1. Confirm the setup and calibration numbers.
  2. Disconnect all bars but one and check its reading. Repeat for each bar. A bar that reads wrong or jumps has a bad load cell or cable (page 27).
